Most of my physics research work asks one question: what symmetries does gravity have? A symmetry is a change you can make to a system that leaves the physics the same, like rotating a perfect sphere. Gravity turns out to have far more of these than anyone expected, hidden at the very edges of space. Studying them is one route to holography, the idea that everything happening inside a region of space can be described by a simpler theory living on its boundary, much like a hologram stores a 3D image on a flat sheet.

Under each paper below is a short summary written for non-specialists. Publication records and citation details are also available through my [INSPIRE-HEP profile](https://inspirehep.net/authors/2148050?ui-citation-summary=true).

1. **S. Banerjee, N. Gupta, S. Misra**
   _Worldsheet CFT<sub>2</sub> and Celestial CFT<sub>2</sub>: An AdS<sub>3</sub>-CFT<sub>2</sub> Perspective._ [Phys. Rev. D 114, 046001](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/hxq4-172y).

   Celestial holography proposes that scattering in flat spacetime can be described by a theory living on the "celestial sphere", the sphere of directions you see when you look up at the night sky. String theory already contains well-understood two-dimensional theories of this kind. We compared the two and found a precise relationship, which gives celestial holography a concrete string-theory footing.

2. **N. Gupta, N. V. Suryanarayana**
   _Chiral Λ-bms<sub>4</sub> Symmetry of 3D Conformal Gravity._ [Eur. Phys. J. C 85 (2025) 4, 468](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-025-14162-5).

   We found the same symmetry as in the paper below in a simpler, three-dimensional model of gravity. Simpler models are where calculations can actually be done, so this gives a workable setting to study it in detail.

3. **N. Gupta, N. V. Suryanarayana**
   _A Chiral Λ-bms<sub>4</sub> Symmetry of AdS<sub>4</sub> Gravity._ [Nucl. Phys. B 1010 (2025) 116759](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2024.116759).

   In flat spacetime, gravity near the boundary has a famous infinite symmetry called BMS. Our universe is not exactly flat, though: it has a cosmological constant. We showed that gravity in four-dimensional anti-de Sitter space also has a version of this symmetry, which links the flat and curved cases.

4. **N. Gupta, N. V. Suryanarayana**
   _All Chiral W-algebra Extensions of so(2,3)._ [J. High Energ. Phys. 2024, 137](<https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP08(2024)137>).

   The universe with a negative cosmological constant (called anti-de Sitter space) has a basic, finite set of symmetries. We asked in how many consistent ways this set can be enlarged into an infinite one, and found the complete list. Think of it as classifying every way a small pattern can be extended into an endless one without breaking its rules.

5. **N. Gupta, P. Paul, N. V. Suryanarayana**
   _sl̂<sub>2</sub> Symmetry of ℝ<sup>1,3</sup> Gravity._ [Phys. Rev. D 108 (2023) 8, 086029](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.108.086029).

   We showed that ordinary gravity in our four-dimensional flat spacetime, looked at in the right way near its boundary, has an infinite family of symmetries of a very well-understood mathematical type. That matters because theories with this kind of symmetry are ones physicists know how to solve.

6. **N. Gupta, N. V. Suryanarayana**
   _Constructing Carrollian CFTs._ [J. High Energ. Phys. 2021, 194](<https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP03(2021)194>).

   Imagine slowing the speed of light down to zero. Nothing can move, yet the resulting physics is surprisingly rich, and it is thought to be the right language for describing flat, ordinary spacetime holographically. We built explicit examples of theories that live in this strange limit, so the idea could be tested on something concrete.
